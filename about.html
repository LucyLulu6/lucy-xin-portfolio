From making objects to shaping experiences

My Journey into Design

My path into UX was not a single decision. It grew from years of making by hand, studying visual culture, building interactive work, and wanting the things I create to solve real problems for people.

Shenzhen → Vancouver

Learning through making

I grew up in Shenzhen and moved to Vancouver for high school. Sewing, baking, and making things by hand helped me recognize an early pattern: I was happiest when I could understand how something was made, then shape the pieces into a finished object of my own.

University of Toronto + Sheridan College

Where art met technology

Art & Art History trained my eye through printmaking, screen printing, copperplate engraving, and relief carving. Alongside it, CCIT—Communication, Culture, Information & Technology—introduced me to web and game design. Watching lines of code become a beautiful, interactive page made technology feel like another material I could create with.

2025

Designing for people

I began the Master of Information in UX Design at the University of Toronto. My focus shifted from making an expressive artifact to understanding what people need, testing whether an idea works, and shaping interfaces that reduce real friction. UX gave my visual and technical background a human purpose.

2026

Building with AI

As AI-assisted coding became part of the design process, I started building my own experiments. I now use vibe coding to move from product question to working prototype faster—learning where AI can support people, where it needs clearer boundaries, and how designers can stay close to the implementation.

Away from Figma

The things that keep me curious

My interests share the same rhythm as my design work: noticing details, understanding how pieces connect, and patiently shaping them into a complete whole.

01

Making

Sewing, crochet, and baking give me the satisfaction of turning raw materials into something useful, warm, and complete.

02

Finding patterns

Puzzles and mystery novels let me follow clues, test possibilities, and enjoy the moment when scattered details finally make sense.

03

Observing

Travel, nature, animals, and photography remind me to look longer and notice quiet moments that might otherwise disappear.

I’m still guided by the same instinct I had when making things by hand: understand the pieces, care about the details, and create something that works as a whole.
